1954 – 1997

The Foundations

When clean tech was a lab curiosity priced like caviar — and a handful of stubborn engineers proved sunlight, wind and electrons could still change the world.

Solar Past

1954

The first practical solar cell

Bell Labs scientists build a silicon cell that turns sunlight into electricity — and celebrate by powering a toy Ferris wheel and a radio transmitter.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 6%conversion efficiency — modern modules reach 23–24%
  • ~$300/Wprice per watt in 1956 — today’s modules cost under $0.40
  • 99.9%cheaper than at launch, adjusted for the same output

Costly as caviar, the cell was still a miracle: for the first time, a machine made sunlight run the modern world. Every rooftop panel today is its descendant.

Bell Labs · Chapin, Fuller & Pearson, 1954

Wind Past

1980

Wind farms take root

California’s mountain passes sprout the world’s first big wind parks, while Danish makers refine turbines from farm machines into power plants.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • $0.55/kWhcost of wind power in the early 1980s — around $0.04 today
  • 90%of the world’s wind capacity sat in California by 1986
  • 15 MWfrom a single modern offshore turbine — 300× the 1980s norm

The first wind rush was noisy, niche and barely profitable. But every blade added taught the industry how to make big machines cheap — and wind never got small again.

IRENA · Danish Energy Agency archives

EV Past

1997

The hybrid that started it all

Toyota launches the Prius in Japan — the first mass-produced hybrid car — and quietly rewires what the auto industry thinks a car can be.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 1997December: the first mass-produced hybrid goes on sale in Japan
  • 20M+electrified Toyota & Lexus cars sold since — the hybrid ripple became a wave
  • 0 gtailpipe CO₂ when the battery does the driving in EV mode

The Prius proved electric drive could be normal, boring, reliable — the highest compliment a car can earn. Twenty-eight years later, plug-in cars owe it a nod.

Toyota Motor Corporation · 1997–2022

2016 – 2024

The Acceleration

When clean tech stopped being the expensive option. Solar beats fossil fuels on price, EVs flood the roads, and AI quietly starts optimising everything it touches.

AI Present

2016

AI checks into the data center

DeepMind’s neural nets take over cooling at Google’s server farms — and slash the energy bill for keeping the cloud chill by double digits.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 40%less energy used for data-center cooling after the AI took over
  • 20%more value squeezed from the same wind farms by ML forecasting
  • <60 sfor GraphCast (2023) to produce a 10-day global forecast

The climate fight is full of tangled systems — weather, grids, traffic, supply chains. Machine learning eats tangled systems for breakfast, and this was the moment it clocked in.

DeepMind · Google, 2016–2023

Green computing Present

2017

Green computing gets serious

Google matches 100% of its electricity use with renewables — and the cloud industry starts chasing carbon-free power for every hour, not just the yearly average.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 100%renewable energy matched by Google’s global operations since 2017
  • 1.1power-usage effectiveness (PUE) at the best hyperscale sites — the industry average is about 1.56
  • 1.5%of the world’s electricity consumed by data centers — the slice AI must now shrink

Computing earned its green stripes the engineer’s way: measure everything, then shave. The 24/7 carbon-free goal now pulls the whole grid toward round-the-clock clean power.

Google Environmental Report · IEA · Uptime Institute

Solar Present

2020

The cheapest electricity in history

The IEA declares utility-scale solar the cheapest source of electricity the world has ever seen, wherever the sun is strong and financing is cheap.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 99.6%cheaper modules than 1976 — from $106 per watt to under $0.40
  • ~600 GWof solar added worldwide in 2024 alone — a new record
  • 23%efficiency now standard in mass-market rooftop panels

A technology curve became an economic law: make it, scale it, watch it get cheaper. Solar crossed from subsidy to bargain — and rewired global energy planning on the way.

IEA World Energy Outlook 2020 · Our World in Data · BNEF

EV Present

2024

The electric decade

One in five new cars sold worldwide now plugs in — up from one in twenty-five just four years earlier. The tailpipe era is ending in real time.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 17M+electric cars sold in 2024 — over 20% of the global market
  • 88.9%of Norway’s new cars in 2024 were fully electric
  • $115/kWhaverage battery pack price — down about 90% since 2010

What began with the Prius went exponential: cheaper batteries, better charging, models for every budget. The electric decade isn’t coming — it’s parked in the driveway.

Rho Motion · OFV Norway · BloombergNEF 2024

2027 – 2050+

The Horizon

When the grid grows a brain, fusion gets a plug, and net-zero stops being a pledge and becomes a date on the calendar. Some of it is already under construction.

Storage Future

2027

Batteries that last 100 hours

Iron-air and sodium-ion cells move from lab benches to substations, storing wind and sunshine for days at a time — not just the evening peak.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 100 hof continuous discharge from iron-air cells — rusting and un-rusting iron
  • 90%fall in lithium-ion pack prices since 2010, still trending down
  • No lithiumneeded for sodium-ion grids — one bottleneck gone

Solar solved cheap; storage is solving dependable. When a week of calm clouds no longer scares the grid, renewables stop needing backup excuses.

Form Energy · BloombergNEF · CATL

Fusion Future

2035

Fusion’s first light

After the 2022 net-energy milestone at the National Ignition Facility, pilot plants race to feed their first real megawatts into actual grids.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 3.15 MJout of a 2.05 MJ input at NIF, December 2022 — fusion’s first net gain
  • 10×energy out vs. heating input — ITER’s design target
  • 10,000 tof coal matched by roughly one kilogram of fusion fuel

Star power, literally. Fusion still has engineering mountains to climb — but the physics has now said yes, and startups signed a deal to supply Microsoft before the decade is out.

LLNL · ITER · Helion–Microsoft PPA

AI Future

2040

AI tends the whole planet

Autonomous grids, kilometre-scale digital twins and self-optimising farms turn Earth-system stewardship into a software problem — with human guardrails.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • 5–10%of global emissions AI could help abate by 2030 — up to 5.3 gigatonnes
  • ~1 kmresolution of Destination Earth, the EU’s digital twin of the planet
  • 24/7self-healing microgrids that reroute around faults in milliseconds

The same technology that routes your commute will dispatch power, steer irrigation and predict droughts. Used well, AI is less a robot overlord than a very attentive gardener.

BCG · EU Destination Earth · IEA grids reports

Net zero Future

2050

The net-zero horizon

On the IEA’s pathway, clean tech is no longer the alternative — it is the default, and the energy system of the 20th century quietly becomes the museum exhibit.

Flip for the numbers

By the numbers

  • ~90%of the world’s electricity from renewables on the net-zero pathway
  • ~70%from solar and wind alone — the 1954 lab curiosity wins big
  • 2035the year zero-emission car sales reach 100% in advanced economies

Every milestone on this timeline was written by people who started before it was fashionable. The next ones are blank on purpose — that’s where you come in.

IEA Net Zero Roadmap · updated 2023
