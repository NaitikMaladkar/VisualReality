How the math works (and where the numbers come from)

Everything is computed in your browser from a small table of static, sourced reference values (approximately 2023–24): each region’s electricity generation, the carbon intensity of its grid, its current generation mix, total territorial CO2 emissions, cars on the road, and an estimate of building-heating emissions.

  • Extra solar / wind adds that share of the region’s generation as clean TWh. It displaces fossil generation first, valued at the fossil intensity (grid emissions ÷ fossil share), which is higher than the grid average. Additions are capped at the fossil share — beyond it, power is curtailed or exported.
  • Electric cars converts a share of the fleet: each petrol car saves its tailpipe emissions (about 2.1 t CO2/yr at 12,000 km), minus the carbon of charging it on the post-plan grid — so green power makes EVs greener, live.
  • Retrofits assumes heat pumps and insulation cut about half of the upgraded homes’ heating emissions.
  • Equivalents: one average coal plant ≈ 3.8 Mt/yr; one petrol car ≈ 2.1 t/yr; a mature tree absorbs ≈ 22 kg/yr; an average home uses ≈ 4.8 MWh/yr.

Sources: Ember Global Electricity Review, IEA, Our World in Data, EPA and IPCC-equivalent factors. The model is deliberately simple — it ignores curtailment dynamics, grid storage, land-use and industry — so treat the outputs as honest ballparks for intuition, not investment forecasts.

Why this matters

Every decimal point is a decision someone hasn’t made yet.
